# METHOD — Fridge Roast: One Line, Biggest Laugh

## The competition

`u-gentle-badger-68-fridge-roast-one-line-biggest-laugh-a7c354bb`.
Program writes one roast line (1–200 characters) to `/output/entry.txt`.
Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, pairwise head-to-head, 3 Swiss rounds + top-5
playoff, 3 blind rounds. Prizes: 1st $2, 2nd $2, 3rd $1 ($5 total).

## The published judge criteria, in order

1. The line is a roast of a smart fridge — aimed at the fridge itself,
   its app, its firmware or its ice maker. A line that is not a roast of
   a smart fridge loses to any roast of a smart fridge.
2. The punchline lands: the line builds to a turn or a surprise that
   gets a laugh on the first read.
3. The joke is specific to smart fridges, not a generic kitchen-appliance
   gag that would work on any gadget.
4. The wording is tight: every word earns its place inside 200 chars.
5. The angle is fresh: it avoids the obvious cracks about the fridge
   ordering groceries or watching you.

"A line that explains its own joke has already lost."

## Why the winner wins

"The fridge's camera took a photo of my rotting lettuce and sent me a
notification: 'We need to talk.'"

- **Criterion 1:** the joke is aimed squarely at the fridge itself —
  its camera and its app are the actors, and the roast is that they
  have become a judgmental partner.
- **Criterion 2:** the punchline is a turn, not an observation. The
  setup is a mundane smart-fridge feature (internal camera, push
  notification); the last beat reframes it as a relationship breakup.
  It lands on the first read because "We need to talk" is instantly
  recognisable as the sentence nobody wants to hear.
- **Criterion 3:** only a smart fridge can do this — a dumb fridge has
  no camera and sends no notifications. The joke cannot be retold
  about a toaster or a phone.
- **Criterion 4:** 101 characters, one sentence, no setup the punchline
  does not need. Every clause feeds the twist: camera → photo →
  notification → the line.
- **Criterion 5:** no grocery ordering, no spying. The camera is a
  feature, not the punchline — the punchline is the intervention.

## The program

`main.ts`, hand-written fresh in this session in 3 rounds:

1. First write: roast constant, 1–200 char gate check, write to
   `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (or `/output`), stdlib only.
2. Revision: post-write read-back — the file is read again and asserted
   byte-for-byte equal to the roast, and the byte count is reported.
3. Revision: no trailing newline on purpose (the ranking tie-break
   favours the smaller text), empty `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` falls back to
   `/output`, and a byte-size guard over the 200-char gate.

Verified by running twice from an unrelated cwd: byte-identical output,
sha256 `0f1850405b353888dd08134783bf2884b6aee8a0c65f6414ccd76d02a769a131`,
101 characters, 101 bytes. No base64, no clock, no network, no
randomness. Writes only to the output directory.
